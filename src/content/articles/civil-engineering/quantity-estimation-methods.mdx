<Key title="Short answer">

**Quantity estimation methods** calculate how much of each item of work a project needs, such as excavation, concrete, brickwork and plaster, from the drawings. For walls and foundations, the three standard methods are the **long wall short wall** method, the **centre line** method and the **crossing** method. Quantities are multiplied by unit rates to give the cost. Measurement follows IS 1200 in India.

</Key>

Before a project can be approved, tendered or built, someone must work out what it will need and what it will cost. Estimation turns drawings and specifications into quantities and money. Good quantity estimates prevent cost overruns, material shortages and disputes, and they are the basis of the bill of quantities on which contractors price their work.

## Types of estimates

- **Preliminary (approximate) estimate:** prepared early to judge feasibility and obtain administrative approval, using costs of similar works.
- **Plinth area estimate:** plinth area × cost per square metre of plinth area for similar construction.
- **Cube rate estimate:** volume of the building × cost per cubic metre; suits buildings of varying heights.
- **Detailed estimate:** every item measured from the drawings, multiplied by rates from a schedule of rates or rate analysis, with contingencies and work charged establishment added. Used for technical sanction and tendering.
- **Revised estimate:** prepared when the sanctioned estimate is likely to be exceeded beyond the permitted limit, or the scope changes.
- **Supplementary estimate:** for additional work found necessary during construction.
- **Annual repair and maintenance estimate:** for keeping a structure in serviceable condition.

## Steps in a detailed estimate

1. **Study the drawings and specifications** and list all items of work in construction sequence.
2. **Take off quantities:** record length, breadth and depth of each item in a measurement sheet.
3. **Abstract the quantities:** total each item and apply the unit of measurement.
4. **Apply rates** from the schedule of rates or a rate analysis.
5. **Add provisions:** contingencies, work charged establishment, taxes, and lump sums for services.
6. **Check:** independent check of key quantities and arithmetic.

### Long wall short wall method

Walls in one direction are treated as **long walls** and measured out to out (centre line length plus one width). Walls in the other direction are **short walls**, measured in to in (centre line length minus one width). Each layer (excavation, foundation concrete, footing steps, plinth wall, superstructure) is worked out with its own width. It is easy to check and handles different wall thicknesses well, so it is widely taught and used.

### Centre line method

The total length of all wall centre lines is found once and multiplied by the breadth and depth of each item. It is fast. At each **T junction**, the centre line length is reduced by half the breadth of the item for every junction, because the cross wall's centre line runs into the main wall. Plain corners need no deduction. The method is most convenient when all walls have the same section.

### Crossing method

Each wall is measured centre line to centre line; at corners and junctions the overlapping or missing volumes are then added or deducted. It is less common in practice.

## Units of measurement

IS 1200 sets how each item is measured and in what unit. Typical units are:

| Item | Unit |
| --- | --- |
| Earthwork excavation, concrete, brickwork | m³ |
| Plastering, flooring, painting, formwork | m² |
| Skirting, pipes, handrails, cornice | m (running) |
| Reinforcement steel | kg or tonne |
| Doors, windows, fittings | number |

Dimensions are usually recorded to the nearest 0.01 m, areas to 0.01 m² and volumes to 0.01 m³, as the relevant part of IS 1200 specifies.

## From quantities to cost: rate analysis

Quantities alone do not give the cost. Each item needs a unit rate, built up by **rate analysis**:

- **Materials:** quantity of each material per unit of work, including wastage, multiplied by its delivered price.
- **Labour:** man days of each trade per unit of work multiplied by wage rates.
- **Equipment and tools:** hire or running cost of mixers, vibrators, scaffolding and similar plant.
- **Overheads and profit:** a percentage added for the contractor's site and head office costs and margin.
- **Water and sundries:** small charges for water, consumables and transport.

Public works departments publish schedules of rates and standard analyses of rates, such as those of CPWD and state PWDs, which are used for government estimates and adapted for private work with current market prices.

## Tips for accurate quantity estimation

- Follow the sequence of construction so no item is forgotten.
- Keep a clear measurement sheet with item number, description, number of members and dimensions.
- Deduct openings in walls and plaster according to the IS 1200 rules, which exempt small openings in some items.
- Apply bulking and dry volume factors when converting concrete and mortar volumes into cement and sand; see [types of cement](/civil-engineering/types-of-cement/) and [water cement ratio](/civil-engineering/water-cement-ratio/).
- Use reduced levels from [levelling in surveying](/civil-engineering/levelling-in-surveying/) for earthwork in roads and plots.
- Use the reinforcement schedule from [limit state design](/civil-engineering/limit-state-design-rcc/) to quantify steel by weight.
