<Key title="Short answer">

**PFD vs P&ID** compares two schematic process drawings. A process flow diagram (PFD) shows major equipment, main streams and a heat and material balance table, describing what the process does. A piping and instrumentation diagram (P&ID) shows every line, valve, instrument and control loop with tags, describing how the plant is built, operated and controlled.

</Key>

Engineers communicate a process plant through drawings. Two of the most important are the PFD and the P&ID. They look similar at first glance, since both are schematic and not to scale, but they serve different purposes and audiences. Knowing what belongs on each, and how to read them, is an essential skill for process, instrumentation, mechanical and operations engineers.

## What is a PFD?

A process flow diagram is produced during process design to describe the process concept. It typically shows:

- Major equipment: vessels, columns, [reactors](/chemical-engineering/cstr-vs-pfr/), heat exchangers, pumps and compressors, with tag numbers and key design data.
- Main process streams with stream numbers, flow direction and connections.
- A **stream table** of flow rates, compositions, temperatures and pressures, taken from the [material balance](/chemical-engineering/material-balance/) and [energy balance](/chemical-engineering/energy-balance/).
- Major control loops at a conceptual level.
- Utilities only where they matter to the process (steam to a reboiler, cooling water to a condenser).

It does **not** show minor lines, isolation valves, drains, vents, instrument details or piping specifications.

## What is a P&ID?

A piping and instrumentation diagram develops the PFD into a complete schematic of the plant for detailed engineering. It shows:

- All equipment, including spares and packages, with tags.
- **All piping**, with line numbers giving size, fluid service, sequence number, piping class and insulation.
- **All valves**: isolation, control, check, relief and manual valves, with their types.
- **All instruments and control loops**, with ISA style tag bubbles indicating function and location (field, control room panel, DCS, safety system).
- Safety devices, interlocks and shutdown systems.
- Vents, drains, sample points, reducers, strainers, spectacle blinds and tie-ins.
- Off-page connectors to link sheets.

## PFD vs P&ID: key differences

| Feature | PFD | P&ID |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose | Show process concept and balances | Show complete piping, instruments and controls |
| Stage | Conceptual and basic design | Detailed design, construction, operation |
| Equipment | Major items only | All items, including spares and packages |
| Lines | Main process streams | Every line with line number and specification |
| Valves | Usually only control valves of interest | All valves, with types |
| Instruments | Key loops, conceptual | Every instrument and loop, fully tagged |
| Process data | Stream table with flows and conditions | Design data in equipment and line lists |
| Main users | Process engineers, management | All disciplines, operators, maintenance, safety reviews (HAZOP) |

## How to read a P&ID

1. **Start with the legend sheets.** They define symbols, line types, abbreviations and the tag format used in that project. Standard symbol sets are summarised in [P&ID symbols](https://www.instrumentationblog.in/tools/pid-symbols/) on InstrumentationBlog.in.
2. **Find the main process path.** Follow the heavy lines from feed to product, equipment by equipment, as on the PFD.
3. **Read equipment tags.** A letter prefix shows type (for example V for vessel, P for pump, E for exchanger, T for tank or tower, depending on the project) and a number shows its unit and sequence.
4. **Read line numbers.** These typically encode size, service, sequence and piping class, for example 2"-P-1003-A1A.
5. **Read instrument bubbles.** The letters give the function; the number is the loop. A circle alone means a field instrument; a line through it means control room panel; a circle in a square indicates a shared display such as a DCS.
6. **Trace control loops.** Follow the dashed signal lines from transmitter to controller to final element, usually a control valve.
7. **Check safety features.** Relief valves, shutdown valves, interlocks and alarms are often shown with special symbols and tags.

## Instrument identification letters (ISA 5.1)

The first letter is the measured or initiating variable; succeeding letters describe functions:

| First letter | Variable | Succeeding letter | Function |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| F | Flow | I | Indicate |
| L | Level | T | Transmit |
| P | Pressure | C | Control |
| T | Temperature | V | Valve |
| A | Analysis | A | Alarm (with H or L for high or low) |
| S | Speed | E | Primary element (sensor) |

Decoding tags quickly gets easier with practice, and an instrument tag decoder helps with less common combinations.

## Who uses which drawing

- **PFDs** are used for process design, debottlenecking studies, operator training overviews and cost estimates.
- **P&IDs** are used by piping, instrumentation, electrical and mechanical engineers in detailed design; by operators and maintenance teams for isolation and permits; and in hazard and operability (HAZOP) studies, where each line and control loop is reviewed node by node.

Both drawings must be kept up to date, and a revision block on each sheet records every change. Every modification in the field should be marked up and incorporated into the as-built P&ID, because operators rely on it to isolate equipment safely. Equipment on both drawings, from [pressure vessels](/chemical-engineering/pressure-vessels-basics/) to [distillation columns](/chemical-engineering/distillation-column-working-principle/) and [heat exchangers](/chemical-engineering/heat-exchanger-types/), is described further in their own articles.

## PFD vs P&ID interview and viva questions

1. **What is the main difference between a PFD and a P&ID?** A PFD shows the process concept with major equipment, main streams and a stream table, while a P&ID shows every line, valve, instrument and control loop needed to build and operate the plant.
2. **Which drawing is produced first?** The PFD, during process design from the heat and material balance; the P&ID is developed from it during detailed engineering.
3. **What do the tags LT-101 and LIC-101 mean?** LT-101 is the level transmitter and LIC-101 the level indicating controller of loop 101, which positions the level control valve LV-101.
4. **What does a line number such as 2"-P-1003-A1A encode?** It typically gives the line size, fluid service, sequence number and piping class, as defined in the project legend sheets.
5. **Why must the P&ID be kept as-built?** Operators and maintenance teams rely on it to isolate equipment safely, and HAZOP studies review it line by line and loop by loop.
