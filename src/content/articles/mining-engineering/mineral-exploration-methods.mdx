<Key title="Short answer">

**Mineral exploration methods** find and define ore deposits in stages. **Target generation** uses regional geology, **remote sensing** and existing data. **Geological mapping** and **geochemical sampling** of rocks, soils and stream sediments locate anomalies. **Geophysical surveys** (magnetic, gravity, electrical, electromagnetic, radiometric and seismic) detect buried bodies with contrasting physical properties. **Drilling** (RC and diamond core) tests targets and provides samples for assays. Results are combined in a **geological model** and **resource estimate**, reported under codes such as **JORC**, **NI 43-101** or the **CIM** standards.

</Key>

Ore deposits are rare. Exploration is a funnel: many prospective areas are screened cheaply, a smaller number receive more detailed and costly work, and very few become mines. Each stage reduces uncertainty and decides whether further spending is justified.

## 1. Target generation and desktop studies

Exploration begins with knowledge of how deposit types form (porphyry copper, orogenic gold, volcanogenic massive sulphide, iron formations and so on) and where favourable geological settings occur. Geologists review published maps, government geophysical and geochemical datasets, old mine records and drill data.

**Remote sensing** from satellites and aircraft maps rock types, structures and alteration minerals: multispectral and hyperspectral imagery can detect clay, iron oxide and other minerals associated with mineralisation. Digital elevation models reveal faults and lineaments.

## 2. Geological mapping and geochemistry

**Field mapping** records rock types, structures, alteration and mineralisation at outcrops, building a geological map. Prospecting and rock chip sampling identify mineralised zones.

**Geochemical exploration** looks for unusual concentrations (anomalies) of target and pathfinder elements:

- **Stream sediment sampling:** covers large catchments cheaply; anomalies point upstream.
- **Soil sampling** on grids to pinpoint sources beneath thin cover.
- **Rock sampling** of outcrops and trenches.
- **Biogeochemistry and hydrogeochemistry** in special settings.

Samples are analysed by methods such as ICP-MS, fire assay for gold, or portable XRF in the field. An anomaly is often defined statistically, for example values above the mean plus two standard deviations of the background.

## 3. Geophysical exploration

Geophysics measures physical property contrasts between ore and host rock, often from aircraft (airborne surveys) over large areas, then on the ground for detail.

| Method | Property measured | Typical targets |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Magnetic | Magnetic susceptibility | Magnetite rich bodies, iron ore, structures, intrusions |
| Gravity | Density | Dense massive sulphides, iron ore, basins |
| Induced polarisation (IP) | Chargeability | Disseminated sulphides, porphyry copper |
| Resistivity | Electrical resistivity | Conductive sulphides, clay alteration, groundwater |
| Electromagnetic (EM) | Conductivity | Massive sulphides, graphite |
| Radiometric | Natural gamma radiation (K, U, Th) | Uranium, potassic alteration, mapping |
| Seismic | Acoustic impedance | Deep structures, coal seams, layered deposits |

Interpretation combines data with geology and increasingly uses 3D inversion modelling and machine learning to rank targets. **Trenching and pitting** expose bedrock where cover is shallow.

## 4. Drilling

Drilling is the only way to confirm a deposit and measure grade at depth.

- **Rotary air blast (RAB) and aircore:** cheap, shallow drilling through soft cover for geochemical sampling.
- **Reverse circulation (RC):** a hammer bit with dual wall rods returns chips; fast and cost effective for grade sampling.
- **Diamond core drilling:** recovers solid core for detailed logging of lithology, structure, alteration, mineralisation and geotechnical data such as RQD, used in [rock mass classification](/mining-engineering/rock-mass-classification/).

Holes are surveyed for direction and depth. Core and chips are logged, split, sampled at regular intervals and assayed in accredited laboratories. **Quality assurance and quality control (QA/QC)** inserts certified standards, blanks and duplicates into sample streams to check accuracy, contamination and precision.

## 5. Geological modelling and resource estimation

Drill data are combined in 3D software to model lithology, structure and mineralised domains. Grades are interpolated into a **block model** using methods such as inverse distance weighting or **kriging**, a geostatistical method that uses the spatial correlation of grades described by the **variogram**. Each block receives a grade and a confidence level.

Resources are classified as **inferred**, **indicated** or **measured** based on data density and geological confidence, and reported under codes such as JORC (Australia), NI 43-101 (Canada) and SAMREC (South Africa), signed off by a **competent or qualified person**.

A simple tonnage estimate for a regular body is:

$$
T = V \times \rho
$$

## Exploration budgets and decisions

Exploration spending is staged so that each step is cheap relative to the information it provides. Regional surveys cost little per square kilometre, while a single diamond drill hole may cost tens of thousands of dollars. Clear criteria, such as anomaly size, geological fit and early drill intercepts, decide whether to advance, pause or drop a target.

## From resource to mine

A promising resource moves through **scoping (preliminary economic assessment)**, **pre-feasibility** and **feasibility studies**, which add metallurgical testwork such as [froth flotation](/mining-engineering/froth-flotation/) trials, mine design choosing between [surface vs underground mining](/mining-engineering/surface-vs-underground-mining/), infrastructure, environmental and social studies including [environmental impact assessment](/environmental-engineering/environmental-impact-assessment/), permitting and economic analysis. Only after these can resources be converted to reserves and a mine built.

## Modern trends in mineral exploration methods

Most easy-to-find outcropping deposits have already been discovered, so explorers increasingly search under cover and at depth. Trends include airborne and drone geophysics, hyperspectral core scanning, portable assay tools for real time decisions in the field, integration of all datasets in 3D, and data science that learns patterns from known deposits. Responsible exploration also means early community engagement, land access agreements and minimal environmental disturbance, with drill pads rehabilitated after use.
