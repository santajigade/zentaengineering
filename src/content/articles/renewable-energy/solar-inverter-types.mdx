<Key title="Short answer">

The main **solar inverter types** are: **string inverters**, which convert power from one or more series strings of panels and are the most common choice for homes and businesses; **central inverters**, large units of hundreds of kW to several MW for utility solar plants; **microinverters**, small inverters mounted behind each panel; **string inverters with power optimisers**, which add DC electronics at each panel; and **hybrid inverters**, which also manage batteries. The choice depends on system size, shading, roof layout, monitoring needs, safety rules and budget.

</Key>

Solar panels produce DC electricity, but homes, factories and the grid use AC. The inverter is the heart of every grid connected [solar PV system](/renewable-energy/how-solar-pv-systems-work/): it converts DC to AC, extracts maximum power from the array, synchronises with the grid and provides protection and monitoring.

## What every PV inverter does

- **DC to AC conversion:** power semiconductors (IGBTs or MOSFETs) switch rapidly in a bridge to create an AC waveform, filtered to a clean sine wave with low [harmonic distortion](/electrical-engineering/power-factor/).
- **Maximum power point tracking (MPPT):** adjusts the operating voltage to extract maximum power as sunlight and temperature change.
- **Grid synchronisation:** matches grid voltage, frequency and phase.
- **Protection:** anti-islanding (disconnects in a grid outage), ground fault and insulation monitoring, over and undervoltage and frequency limits, and arc fault detection in some markets.
- **Grid support:** modern inverters can control reactive power, ride through faults and reduce output if frequency rises, as required by grid codes such as IEEE 1547.
- **Monitoring and communication:** energy logging and remote diagnostics.

## The PV operating point

A PV array's current falls sharply as voltage approaches open circuit. Power, the product P = V × I, peaks at the **maximum power point** (V_mp, I_mp). MPPT algorithms keep the array there. One MPPT input works well only if all panels on it see similar conditions; a shaded panel in a string drags the whole string down, which is why inverter architecture matters.

## Solar inverter types in detail

### String inverters

Panels are wired in series into strings of perhaps 8 to 25 modules, giving a few hundred to 1000 V or more DC. One string inverter, typically 2 to 350 kW, handles one or several strings, often with two or more MPPT inputs so that strings facing different directions can be tracked separately.

- **Pros:** low cost per watt, high efficiency (about 97 to 99%), easy to service at ground level, proven.
- **Cons:** shading or mismatch on one panel affects the string; high DC voltage on the roof; monitoring only at string level.

### Central inverters

Large inverters from several hundred kW to over 5 MW, fed by many strings through **combiner boxes**, usually in a container with a medium voltage transformer.

- **Pros:** lowest cost per watt for large plants, few units to maintain, strong grid support capability.
- **Cons:** a fault takes a large block offline; long DC cable runs; limited MPPT granularity. Many utility plants now use large string inverters instead for redundancy.

### Microinverters

A small inverter, typically 300 to 800 W, under each panel (or pair of panels) converts DC to AC on the roof, so only AC cabling runs to the switchboard.

- **Pros:** independent MPPT for every panel, so shading on one panel does not affect others; panel level monitoring; low DC voltage improves fire safety and simplifies rapid shutdown rules; easy to expand.
- **Cons:** higher cost per watt; more electronics on the roof exposed to heat; replacements require roof access.

### Power optimisers

DC to DC converters fitted to each panel perform module level MPPT and pass conditioned DC to a central string inverter.

- **Pros:** most of the shading and monitoring benefits of microinverters, with a single inverter; supports rapid shutdown.
- **Cons:** more components than a plain string system; still needs the central inverter.

### Hybrid inverters

A hybrid (multimode) inverter combines PV MPPT inputs with a battery charger and inverter. It can store solar energy, supply backup power in outages, and manage grid import and export. See [battery energy storage systems](/renewable-energy/battery-energy-storage-systems/).

## Efficiency

Peak efficiency figures can reach 99%, but the **weighted efficiency** (European or CEC efficiency), which averages performance over typical loading, is more meaningful. Inverter heat raises losses, so they should be installed in shaded, ventilated places.

## String sizing

String voltage must stay below the inverter's maximum DC input at the coldest expected temperature, because panel voltage rises in the cold:

$$
V_{oc,\text{cold}} = V_{oc,\text{STC}}\,\big[1 + \beta\,(T_\text{min} - 25)\big]
$$

where β is the voltage temperature coefficient (negative, per °C).

<Example>
<dl>
<dt>Given</dt><dd>Panels with V_oc = 49.5 V at STC and β = −0.27%/°C. Minimum site temperature 0 °C. Inverter maximum DC input 1000 V.</dd>
<dt>Find</dt><dd>The maximum number of panels per string.</dd>
<dt>Assumptions</dt><dd>Cell temperature equals ambient at sunrise on the coldest day.</dd>
<dt>Solution</dt><dd>V_oc,cold = 49.5 × [1 + (−0.0027)(0 − 25)] = 49.5 × 1.0675 = 52.8 V<br />Max panels = 1000 ÷ 52.8 = 18.9, round down to 18</dd>
<dt>Answer</dt><dd><strong>Up to 18 panels per string (about 951 V at the coldest condition)</strong></dd>
<dt>Limitations</dt><dd>Also check that the string's operating voltage at high temperature stays above the inverter's minimum MPPT voltage.</dd>
</dl>
</Example>

## Choosing among solar inverter types

Consider system size, shading and roof complexity, local safety codes such as rapid shutdown requirements, need for batteries or backup, warranty length (often 10 to 12 years for string inverters and 20 to 25 years for microinverters), service access and total cost over the system life. Good design and correct [cable sizing](/electrical-engineering/cable-sizing/) on both DC and AC sides matter as much as the inverter itself.
