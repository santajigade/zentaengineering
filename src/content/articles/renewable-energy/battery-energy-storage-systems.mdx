<Key title="Short answer">

**Battery energy storage systems** (BESS) store electrical energy in rechargeable batteries and release it when needed. A BESS combines **battery cells** grouped into modules and racks, a **battery management system (BMS)** that protects and balances them, a **power conversion system (PCS)** of bidirectional inverters, an **energy management system (EMS)** that decides when to charge and discharge, plus cooling, fire protection and transformers. Systems are rated by **power (MW)** and **energy (MWh)**. Uses include solar shifting, peak shaving, frequency regulation, backup power and microgrids.

</Key>

Electricity must be generated at the same moment it is used. As solar and wind provide more of our power, storage becomes essential to balance supply and demand. Batteries are now the fastest growing form of grid storage, ranging from a few kWh in homes to several GWh in utility plants.

### Battery cells, modules and racks

Cells are connected in series and parallel into **modules**, and modules into **racks** or **strings** reaching DC voltages of about 1000 to 1500 V. Large systems are delivered in containers of around 3 to 5 MWh each.

### Battery management system (BMS)

The BMS monitors cell voltages, currents and temperatures; estimates **state of charge (SoC)** and **state of health (SoH)**; balances cells; and opens contactors to protect against overvoltage, undervoltage, overcurrent and overtemperature.

### Power conversion system (PCS)

Bidirectional inverters convert DC from the batteries to AC for the grid and AC back to DC for charging. Grid forming inverters can also set voltage and frequency, which is vital for [microgrids](/renewable-energy/microgrid-explained/) and weak grids. The power electronics work on the same switching principles as a [buck converter](/electronics-engineering/buck-converter/) and inverter bridges.

### Energy management system (EMS)

The EMS is the brain: it receives market prices, forecasts, grid operator signals and site load data and schedules charging and discharging to meet the application's goals.

### Balance of plant

Thermal management (air conditioning or liquid cooling) keeps cells in their ideal range, typically about 15 to 35 °C. Fire detection, gas detection, explosion venting and suppression systems address the risk of **thermal runaway**. Transformers, switchgear, protection relays and SCADA complete the plant.

## Battery chemistries

| Chemistry | Strengths | Limitations | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lithium iron phosphate (LFP) | Safe, long life, low cost, no cobalt | Lower energy density | Most grid and home storage |
| Lithium NMC | High energy density | Higher cost, less thermally stable | EVs, some stationary |
| Lead acid | Cheap, recyclable, mature | Short cycle life, heavy | UPS, small off-grid |
| Sodium-ion | Abundant materials, good low temperature | Lower energy density, emerging | Grid storage, budget EVs |
| Flow batteries (vanadium) | Very long cycle life, power and energy scale separately | Lower efficiency, bulky | Long duration storage |

## Key ratings

- **Power (MW):** maximum charge or discharge rate.
- **Energy (MWh):** storage capacity. Energy divided by power gives **duration**, typically 1 to 4 hours for lithium systems.
- **Round trip efficiency:** energy out divided by energy in, about 85 to 90% AC to AC for lithium-ion.
- **Depth of discharge (DoD):** the share of capacity used; limiting DoD extends life.
- **C-rate:** current relative to capacity; 0.5C means a full charge or discharge in 2 hours.
- **Cycle life and degradation:** capacity fades with cycles and calendar time.

The usable energy needed for a given load is:

$$
E_\text{nominal} = \frac{P \times t}{DoD \times \eta}
$$

## Applications of battery energy storage systems

- **Solar and wind shifting:** store midday solar for the evening peak; smooth fast variations from [solar PV systems](/renewable-energy/how-solar-pv-systems-work/) and [wind turbines](/renewable-energy/how-wind-turbines-work/).
- **Frequency regulation:** batteries respond within milliseconds, faster than any power station, to stabilise grid frequency.
- **Peak shaving and demand charge reduction** for industrial and commercial users.
- **Energy arbitrage:** charge when prices are low, discharge when high.
- **Backup power and resilience:** keep critical loads running during outages.
- **Transmission and distribution deferral:** relieve congested lines instead of upgrading them.
- **EV charging support:** buffer the grid connection at fast charging sites, as discussed in [EV charging levels and connectors](/renewable-energy/ev-charging-levels-and-connectors/).

## Battery energy storage systems at home and in industry

Home systems of about 5 to 20 kWh pair with rooftop solar and a hybrid inverter, storing daytime surplus for the evening and providing backup during outages. Commercial and industrial systems of a few hundred kWh to several MWh mainly cut demand charges, support EV charging and improve power quality. Utility systems of tens to thousands of MWh provide grid services and shift renewable energy at scale. The building blocks are the same; the control strategy and revenue model differ.

## Degradation and life

Batteries lose capacity through cycling and with time. High temperature, high state of charge held for long periods, deep cycles and high currents all speed up ageing. Operators manage this by keeping cells cool, limiting the usable state of charge window, and planning augmentation, adding new modules after some years to restore the contracted energy capacity. Recycling recovers lithium, nickel, cobalt, copper and aluminium at end of life.

## Safety

Lithium-ion cells can enter **thermal runaway** if damaged, overcharged or overheated: internal reactions release heat and flammable gases, which can spread to neighbouring cells. Safety relies on quality cells, a robust BMS, good thermal design, spacing between units, gas detection, deflagration venting, and fire protection designed to standards such as NFPA 855 and tested under UL 9540A. Emergency response plans and training for local fire services are also part of good practice.

Battery storage turns variable renewable output into dependable power and is now a core building block of modern electricity systems.
