<Key title="Short answer">

**Well logging methods** record rock and fluid properties continuously along a borehole using tools lowered on a cable (**wireline**) or built into the drill string (**logging while drilling**, LWD). The main logs are **gamma ray** (natural radioactivity, to tell shale from clean rock), **resistivity** (to detect hydrocarbons versus salt water), **density** and **neutron** (to measure porosity) and **sonic** (acoustic travel time, for porosity and rock mechanics). Combined, they tell engineers where the reservoir is, how porous it is and what fluids it contains.

</Key>

Cores give direct samples, but they are expensive and cover only short intervals. Well logs measure the whole well, foot by foot, within hours of drilling. They are the main source of data for deciding which zones to test, perforate and produce, and for building the reservoir model.

## How logging is done

- **Wireline logging:** after drilling a section, a string of sondes is lowered on an armoured electric cable. Measurements are recorded as the tool is pulled up at a steady speed and transmitted to a surface logging unit.
- **Logging while drilling (LWD):** sensors in drill collars just above the bit measure as the well is drilled and send selected data to surface by mud pulse telemetry, storing the full data in memory.
- **Cased hole logging:** tools run inside steel casing to check cement quality, corrosion and production profiles.

Logs are plotted against depth in tracks, typically gamma ray on the left, resistivity in the middle and porosity logs on the right.

## Main well logging methods

### Gamma ray log

Measures natural gamma radiation from potassium, thorium and uranium in the rock, in API units. Shales are radioactive; clean sandstones and carbonates are not. The gamma ray log is used to identify lithology, pick formation tops, estimate shale volume and correlate between wells. **Spectral gamma ray** tools separate the three elements for better mineral identification.

### Spontaneous potential (SP) log

Records natural voltage differences between the borehole mud and formation water. It helps identify permeable beds and estimate formation water resistivity, but only works in water based mud.

### Resistivity logs

Measure how strongly the formation resists electric current, in ohm metres (Ω·m).

- **Laterolog** tools focus current into the formation and work best in salty, conductive mud.
- **Induction** tools induce eddy currents with coils and work in oil based or fresh mud.
- Tools read at several **depths of investigation**: shallow readings see the zone invaded by mud filtrate; deep readings see the undisturbed formation, giving true resistivity Rt.

High deep resistivity in a porous zone usually indicates hydrocarbons.

### Density log

A gamma ray source sends radiation into the formation; detectors count the scattered rays, which depend on electron density and therefore bulk density ρ_b. Porosity follows from:

$$
\phi_D = \frac{\rho_{ma} - \rho_b}{\rho_{ma} - \rho_f}
$$

where ρ_ma is the matrix density (2.65 g/cm³ for sandstone, 2.71 for limestone) and ρ_f the fluid density.

### Neutron log

A neutron source bombards the formation; hydrogen atoms slow the neutrons most effectively. Since hydrogen is mainly in pore fluids, the log responds to porosity. Gas has low hydrogen density, so neutron porosity reads low in gas zones while density porosity reads high: this **crossover** is a classic gas indicator.

### Sonic log

Measures the travel time of a sound wave through the formation, Δt, in microseconds per foot. Slower travel means more porosity. Sonic data also gives rock strength and elastic properties for drilling and fracturing design, and ties wells to seismic surveys.

### Advanced logs

Nuclear magnetic resonance (pore size and free fluid), borehole imaging (fractures and bedding), formation pressure testers and fluid samplers complement the basic suite.

## Well logging methods compared

| Log | Measures | Main use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gamma ray | Natural radioactivity | Shale vs sand, correlation |
| SP | Natural electrical potential | Permeable beds, Rw |
| Resistivity | Electrical resistance | Hydrocarbon vs water |
| Density | Bulk density | Porosity, lithology |
| Neutron | Hydrogen content | Porosity, gas detection |
| Sonic | Acoustic travel time | Porosity, rock mechanics, seismic tie |

## Water saturation and Archie's equation

G. E. Archie showed in 1942 that for clean rocks, water saturation S_w can be calculated from resistivity and porosity:

$$
S_w = \left(\frac{a\,R_w}{\phi^m\,R_t}\right)^{1/n}
$$

with a ≈ 1, m ≈ 2 and n ≈ 2 for many sandstones. Hydrocarbon saturation is 1 − S_w.

<dt>Find</dt><dd>Porosity, water saturation and hydrocarbon saturation.</dd>
<dt>Assumptions</dt><dd>Clean (shale free) sand; Archie parameters valid.</dd>
<dt>Solution</dt><dd>φ = (2.65 − 2.32) ÷ (2.65 − 1.0) = 0.33 ÷ 1.65 = 0.20<br />S_w = √(1 × 0.05 ÷ (0.20² × 20)) = √(0.05 ÷ 0.8) = √0.0625 = 0.25<br />S_h = 1 − 0.25 = 0.75</dd>
<dt>Answer</dt><dd><strong>Porosity 20%, water saturation 25%, hydrocarbon saturation 75%: a promising hydrocarbon zone</strong></dd>
<dt>Limitations</dt><dd>Shaly sands need modified equations; Archie parameters should be confirmed with core measurements.</dd>

## Practical challenges

Log quality depends on borehole conditions. Washed out or rough holes spoil density and neutron readings, which need good pad contact. Mud filtrate invasion alters shallow resistivity readings, so deep reading tools and invasion corrections are used. Tool calibration, depth matching between runs and environmental corrections for mud weight, hole size and temperature are all part of preparing logs for interpretation. Radioactive sources in density and neutron tools require strict handling procedures, and pulsed neutron generators are increasingly used instead of chemical sources.

## Well logging methods in the digital era

Log data is now delivered digitally in standard formats such as LAS and DLIS, interpreted with specialist software, and increasingly combined with machine learning to predict missing curves or classify rock types across many wells.

## Using the results

Log interpretation identifies net pay thickness, porosity and saturation, which together with area give hydrocarbons in place. Logs also guide where to set casing, which intervals to perforate, and how to model [porosity and permeability](/petroleum-engineering/porosity-and-permeability/) across the field. Logs are run from the [drilling rig](/petroleum-engineering/rotary-drilling-rig-components/), and later production logs help diagnose wells that need [artificial lift](/petroleum-engineering/artificial-lift-methods/) or water shut off.
