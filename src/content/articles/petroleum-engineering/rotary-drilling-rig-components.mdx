  - q: "What is the difference between a kelly and a top drive?"
    a: "A kelly is a square or hexagonal pipe turned by a rotary table on the rig floor; drilling stops every 9 to 12 m to add a joint of pipe.   - q: "Why is drilling mud so important?"
    a: "Drilling fluid carries rock cuttings to the surface, cools and lubricates the bit, supports the borehole wall, and most importantly provides hydrostatic pressure that holds back formation fluids. If mud weight is too low, oil, gas or water can enter the well and cause a kick; if too high, it can fracture the formation and cause losses."

<Key title="Short answer">

**Rotary drilling rig components** are grouped into five systems. The **hoisting system** (derrick or mast, drawworks, crown and travelling blocks) lifts and lowers the drill string. The **rotating system** (top drive or rotary table and kelly) turns it. The **circulating system** (mud pumps, standpipe, mud tanks and solids control equipment) pumps drilling fluid down the pipe and back up the annulus. The **power system** supplies energy, and the **well control system** (blowout preventer and choke manifold) keeps formation fluids under control. The **drill string** and **bit** do the actual cutting.

</Key>

Almost every oil and gas well, and many geothermal and water wells, is drilled by rotary drilling. A bit at the bottom of a long string of steel pipe is turned and pressed into the rock while fluid is circulated to remove the cuttings. A land rig may drill wells 3000 to 6000 m deep; offshore rigs reach over 10 000 m of measured depth. Understanding each component explains how this remarkable machine works.

## 1. Hoisting system

The hoisting system raises and lowers the drill string, casing and tools, and controls the weight applied to the bit.

- **Derrick or mast:** the steel tower that supports the blocks. Its height (often 40 to 50 m) sets how long a stand of pipe can be racked.
- **Drawworks:** a large winch with a drum, brakes and motor that reels the **drilling line** (wire rope) in and out.
- **Crown block:** fixed sheaves at the top of the derrick.
- **Travelling block and hook:** moving sheaves that hang from the drilling line and carry the top drive or swivel. Reeving the line through several sheaves multiplies the lifting force.
- **Deadline anchor:** holds the fixed end of the line and carries a load cell that measures **hook load**.

## 2. Rotating system

- **Top drive:** an electric or hydraulic motor suspended from the travelling block that turns the drill string from the top. It allows drilling with stands of three joints and makes connections faster and safer.
- **Rotary table and kelly:** the older arrangement, where a turntable in the drill floor rotates a square or hexagonal kelly attached to the top of the string.
- **Downhole motors:** mud motors and rotary steerable systems turn or steer the bit using mud flow, essential for directional and horizontal wells.

## 3. Circulating system

Drilling fluid, or **mud**, is pumped continuously:

1. **Mud pumps**, usually triplex piston pumps, deliver high pressure mud (often 200 to 350 bar).
2. Mud travels up the **standpipe**, through the rotary hose and top drive, and down inside the drill string.
3. It exits through **nozzles in the bit**, cleaning and cooling it.
4. It returns up the **annulus**, the gap between the drill string and the hole wall, carrying cuttings.
5. At the surface, **shale shakers**, desanders, desilters and centrifuges remove solids.
6. Clean mud returns to the **mud tanks** for treatment and recirculation.

Mud density controls well pressure, while viscosity and gel strength control hole cleaning. Return flow and pit volumes are closely monitored because a gain signals a possible kick. Accurate [flow measurement](https://www.instrumentationblog.in/category/process-instrumentation/flow-measurement/) and [level measurement](https://www.instrumentationblog.in/category/process-instrumentation/level-measurement/) in the pits are key safety instruments.

The hydrostatic pressure of the mud column at depth h is:

$$
P = \rho g h
$$

## 4. Power system

Most rigs use diesel engines driving generators, with variable frequency drives powering the drawworks, top drive and mud pumps. Some rigs run on grid power or gas engines to cut emissions and fuel cost.

## 5. Well control system

- **Blowout preventer (BOP) stack:** annular preventer, pipe rams, blind rams and shear rams mounted on the wellhead.
- **Accumulator unit:** stores hydraulic energy to close the BOP within seconds, even without rig power.
- **Choke and kill manifold:** controls flow from the well and allows heavy mud to be pumped in to regain control.
- **Mud gas separator:** removes gas from returning mud safely.

## Drill string and bit

- **Drill pipe:** jointed steel pipe, typically about 9.5 m long, connected by threaded tool joints.
- **Bottom hole assembly (BHA):** heavy **drill collars** that put weight on the bit, stabilisers, measurement while drilling (MWD) and logging while drilling tools.
- **Bits:** **PDC bits** with fixed polycrystalline diamond cutters shear rock and dominate most drilling today; **roller cone (tricone) bits** crush and gouge harder or mixed formations.

## Rotary drilling rig components for automation and safety

Modern rigs add a layer of automation on top of the mechanical systems. Pipe handling robots and iron roughnecks make and break connections, keeping crews out of the most dangerous zone on the drill floor. The driller works from a cabin with joystick controls and screens showing hook load, weight on bit, torque, pump pressure, flow and pit volumes. Software can automate drilling parameters and trip speeds to limit pressure surges in the well. Sensors on every key component support condition monitoring of top drives, drawworks and mud pumps.

## Types of rotary rigs

- **Land rigs:** truck mounted, skid or walking rigs for onshore wells.
- **Jack-up rigs:** legs stand on the seabed in shallow water, up to about 120 to 150 m.
- **Semi-submersibles and drillships:** floating rigs for deep water, using subsea BOPs and riser systems.
- **Platform rigs:** installed on fixed production platforms, described in [offshore platform types](/petroleum-engineering/offshore-platform-types/).

Drilling delivers access to the reservoir whose [porosity and permeability](/petroleum-engineering/porosity-and-permeability/) decide its value, and the cuttings and logs gathered while drilling are the first real data about the rock. Once the well is drilled, it is evaluated with [well logging](/petroleum-engineering/well-logging-methods/) and completed for production.
