<Key title="Short answer">

**SOLAS** (the International Convention for the Safety of Life at Sea) sets mandatory safety equipment for merchant ships. **Chapter III and the LSA Code** cover **lifesaving appliances**: **lifeboats** (davit launched or **free-fall**), **liferafts** (inflatable, with **hydrostatic release units**), **rescue boats**, **lifejackets**, **immersion suits**, **lifebuoys**, **EPIRBs**, **SARTs**, **pyrotechnics** and **muster and embarkation** arrangements. **Chapter II-2 and the FSS Code** cover **fire safety**: structural fire protection, **fire detection and alarm**, **fire mains, pumps and hydrants**, **fixed firefighting systems** (CO₂, high expansion foam, water mist, sprinklers), **portable extinguishers**, **fireman's outfits** and **EEBDs**. Regular **drills, inspections and maintenance** keep everything ready.

</Key>

SOLAS was first adopted in 1914 after the Titanic disaster and has been updated many times, most significantly in 1974. It is widely regarded as the most important international treaty on merchant ship safety. Flag states, classification societies and port state control inspectors check that ships comply.

## Lifesaving appliances (SOLAS Chapter III, LSA Code)

### Survival craft

- **Lifeboats:** totally enclosed, self righting boats with engines, provisions, water and equipment. **Davit launched** lifeboats are lowered from each side; **free-fall** lifeboats slide from a stern ramp and drop into the sea, allowing fast launching even with list or trim. Tankers carry fire protected lifeboats with water spray and air supply.
- **Liferafts:** inflatable rafts packed in canisters, inflated by CO₂ gas, either thrown overboard or launched by davit. A **hydrostatic release unit** frees the canister automatically if the ship sinks, at a depth of about 4 m.
- **Rescue boats:** fast boats for recovering people in the water and marshalling survival craft.

### Personal lifesaving appliances

- **Lifejackets** for every person, plus extras, with lights and whistles; **infant and child sizes** on passenger ships.
- **Immersion suits** and **thermal protective aids** to prevent hypothermia.
- **Lifebuoys** around the ship, some with self igniting lights, smoke signals and lines.

### Communication and location

- **EPIRB** (emergency position indicating radio beacon): floats free and transmits a distress signal with position via the COSPAS-SARSAT satellite system, using satellite links like those in [GEO vs MEO vs LEO satellites](/telecommunication-engineering/geo-meo-leo-satellites/).
- **SART** or **AIS-SART**: shows survival craft position on rescue vessels' radar or AIS.
- **Two way VHF radios** for survival craft.
- **Pyrotechnics:** rocket parachute flares, hand flares and buoyant smoke signals.
- **Line throwing appliance.**

### Muster and embarkation

Muster stations, embarkation ladders, marine evacuation systems on passenger ships, emergency lighting and clear signage support orderly abandonment. The **general emergency alarm** is seven or more short blasts followed by one long blast.

## Fire safety (SOLAS Chapter II-2, FSS Code)

### Prevention and structural fire protection

Ships are divided into **main vertical zones** and spaces separated by fire rated divisions: **A class** (steel, insulated to stop fire for 60 minutes), **B class** and **C class**. Fire doors, dampers and penetrations maintain integrity. Fuel oil systems have shielded high pressure pipes and spray shields to prevent leaks reaching hot surfaces, which are insulated, since engine room oil fires are the most common serious ship fires.

### Detection and alarm

**Fixed fire detection and alarm systems** with smoke and heat detectors and manual call points cover accommodation, service spaces, control stations and unmanned machinery spaces. Detector types and their uses are described in [types of fire detectors](/safety-engineering/types-of-fire-detectors/).

### Fire main, pumps and hydrants

At least two independently driven **main fire pumps** (in the engine room) and an **emergency fire pump** located outside the machinery space supply the **fire main** with hydrants, hoses and dual purpose (jet and spray) nozzles placed so that every part of the ship can be reached by at least two jets.

### Fixed firefighting systems

- **CO₂ total flooding** for engine rooms, cargo holds and pump rooms, released only after people are evacuated and the space is sealed, with pre-discharge alarms.
- **High expansion foam** and **water based systems** (water mist, local application systems for high risk areas such as engine tops and purifiers).
- **Automatic sprinklers** in accommodation on passenger ships.
- **Deck foam systems** and **inert gas** on tankers.
- **Galley fryer extinguishing systems.**

### Portable equipment and protective gear

Portable and semi-portable extinguishers chosen for the [fire classes](/safety-engineering/fire-classes-and-extinguishers/) likely in each space, **fireman's outfits** with breathing apparatus, fire blankets, and **emergency escape breathing devices (EEBDs)** in accommodation and machinery spaces.

## Drills, training and maintenance

SOLAS requires monthly **abandon ship** and **fire drills** for crew on cargo ships, with lifeboats lowered and launched at specified intervals, enclosed space entry and rescue drills, and familiarisation training for new crew. Equipment must be inspected weekly and monthly, serviced annually by approved service providers (liferafts, hydrostatic release units, CO₂ systems, lifeboat release gear), and tested to the LSA and FSS Codes. Records are kept for inspections by flag and port state.

## SOLAS in the wider safety system

Lifesaving and firefighting equipment is the last line of defence. SOLAS also covers ship construction and [stability](/marine-engineering/ship-stability-basics/), machinery and [electrical installations](/marine-engineering/ship-electrical-power-system/) including emergency power, navigation safety and the ISM Code for safety management. Combined with risk assessment tools such as a [risk matrix](/safety-engineering/risk-matrix/), good maintenance and well trained crews, SOLAS equipment gives seafarers the best possible chance of surviving an emergency at sea.
