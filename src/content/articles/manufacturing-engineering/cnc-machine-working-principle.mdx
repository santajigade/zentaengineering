<Key title="Short answer">

A **CNC machine** (computer numerical control) moves a cutting tool or workpiece automatically along programmed paths. CAM software generates G-code, and the CNC controller sends commands to servo drives and motors that turn ball screws to move the axes. Encoders and linear scales feed back position in a closed loop while the spindle turns the tool or workpiece.

</Key>

CNC machines brought the accuracy and repeatability of computers to metal cutting. They produce parts within micrometres, run unattended for hours, and switch between jobs simply by loading a new program. They are the backbone of modern machining, from small job shops to aerospace and automotive production lines.

## How a CNC machine works

1. **Design:** the part is modelled in CAD.
2. **CAM programming:** CAM software calculates tool paths for each operation, considering tools, cutting speeds, feeds and stock, and a **post processor** outputs G-code for the specific machine and controller.
3. **Setup:** the operator fixes the workpiece in a vice or fixture, loads tools, measures tool lengths and sets **work offsets** that link the program's origin to the part.
4. **Execution:** the controller reads each block, plans motion with acceleration limits and look-ahead, and continuously sends position commands to the axis drives.
5. **Feedback and correction:** encoders on motors, and often linear scales on axes, report actual positions; the servo loop corrects any difference thousands of times per second.
6. **Inspection:** parts are checked with gauges, probes or a coordinate measuring machine.

## Main parts of a CNC machine

- **Machine structure (bed, column, saddle):** cast iron, welded steel or polymer concrete, designed for stiffness and vibration damping.
- **CNC controller:** industrial computer with the HMI, program memory, motion planning (interpolation), PLC for machine logic, and drive interfaces. Common brands include Fanuc, Siemens, Heidenhain, Mitsubishi and Haas.
- **Axis drives and motors:** AC **servo motors** with encoders give fast, precise motion; linear motors are used in high speed machines. See [servo vs stepper motor](/automation-robotics/servo-vs-stepper-motor/).
- **Ball screws and linear guides:** convert rotation into smooth, low friction linear motion with minimal backlash.
- **Spindle:** a precision motorised shaft holding the tool (mill) or workpiece (lathe), with speeds from a few hundred to over 30 000 rpm.
- **Automatic tool changer (ATC):** a magazine or turret that swaps tools in seconds.
- **Workholding:** vices, chucks, fixtures, pallets and rotary tables.
- **Coolant and chip management:** pumps, nozzles, chip conveyors and filtration.
- **Probing and measurement:** touch probes for setting work offsets and checking parts, tool setters for length and wear.
- **Enclosure and safety interlocks.**

## Axes and coordinate system

Axes follow ISO 841: **Z** is along the spindle axis, **X** is usually the longest horizontal travel, and **Y** completes a right handed system. Rotary axes **A, B and C** rotate about X, Y and Z. A 3-axis mill moves X, Y, Z; 4-axis adds one rotary axis; 5-axis adds two, allowing complex surfaces in one setup.

## G-code basics

A short milling program fragment:

- `G21 G90 G17` metric units, absolute coordinates, XY plane
- `T1 M06` load tool 1
- `S2000 M03` spindle 2000 rpm clockwise
- `G00 X0 Y0 Z5` rapid move above the start
- `G01 Z-2 F150` feed down 2 mm at 150 mm/min
- `G01 X50 F400` cut a straight line
- `G02 X60 Y10 R10` cut a clockwise arc
- `M05 M30` spindle stop, program end

## Cutting speed and feed

Spindle speed comes from the recommended **cutting speed** V_c for the tool and material:

$$
N = \frac{1000\,V_c}{\pi\,D}
$$

where N is in rpm, V_c in m/min and D the tool (or workpiece) diameter in mm. Feed rate for milling is:

$$
V_f = f_z \times z \times N
$$

where f_z is feed per tooth and z the number of teeth.

## Types of CNC machines

- **CNC milling machines and machining centres** (vertical and horizontal), the most common.
- **CNC lathes and turning centres**, often with live tooling and a sub spindle (mill-turn).
- **CNC routers** for wood, plastics and composites.
- **CNC grinders** for precision finishing.
- **Wire and sinker EDM** for hard materials and intricate shapes.
- **Laser, plasma and waterjet cutters** for sheet and plate.
- **CNC press brakes** for bending.

## Advantages and limitations of a CNC machine

**Advantages:** high accuracy and repeatability, complex shapes, quick changeover between jobs, reduced reliance on operator skill, unattended running, and digital integration with CAD/CAM and production systems.

**Limitations:** high capital cost, need for skilled programmers and maintenance, and machining wastes material as chips. CNC machining remains a core subtractive process among the [manufacturing processes](/manufacturing-engineering/manufacturing-processes-classification/), increasingly combined with [3D printing](/manufacturing-engineering/3d-printing-technologies/) for hybrid production.

## Programming methods

Programs can be written by hand for simple parts, created with **conversational programming** at the control, where the operator fills in prompts for pockets, holes and contours, or generated by **CAM** software for complex 3D shapes. Simulation in CAM or on the control checks for collisions and gouges before cutting. **Canned cycles** such as G81 for drilling and G83 for peck drilling shorten programs for repeated operations, and **cutter radius compensation** (G41 and G42) lets programs follow part geometry while the control offsets for tool size.

## Maintenance and accuracy

Accuracy depends on regular maintenance: lubrication of guides and screws, spindle bearing checks, backlash compensation, ballbar tests for circularity and laser calibration of axis positioning. Thermal growth of the spindle and structure is a major error source, so warm-up cycles and thermal compensation are used. Planned [types of maintenance](/manufacturing-engineering/types-of-maintenance/) such as preventive and predictive programmes keep machines productive.

## CNC machine interview and viva questions

1. **What is closed loop control in a CNC machine?** Encoders on the motors, and often linear scales on the axes, report actual position, and the servo loop corrects any difference from the commanded position thousands of times per second.
2. **What does a post processor do?** It converts the CAM tool paths into G-code suited to the specific machine and controller.
3. **How are the X, Y and Z axes defined?** Under ISO 841, Z is along the spindle axis, X is usually the longest horizontal travel and Y completes a right handed system.
4. **What is the difference between G00 and G01?** G00 is a rapid positioning move, while G01 is a straight line cutting move at the programmed feed rate.
5. **What is the purpose of cutter radius compensation?** G41 and G42 let the program follow the part geometry while the control offsets the path for the tool size.
