<Key title="Short answer">

**Six Sigma DMAIC** is a structured, data driven method for improving an existing process by reducing variation and defects. Its five phases are Define the problem, Measure current performance, Analyse root causes, Improve the process and Control the gains. A six sigma process produces about 3.4 defects per million opportunities.

</Key>

Six Sigma was developed at Motorola in the 1980s and made famous by General Electric in the 1990s. It treats quality problems as statistical problems: if the causes of variation in a process are understood and controlled, defects fall. DMAIC is its core problem solving roadmap, and ISO 13053 standardises it.

## Sigma level and DPMO

Sigma (σ) is the standard deviation of a process. The **sigma level** expresses how many standard deviations fit between the process mean and the nearest specification limit, after allowing for long term drift. Performance is often measured as **defects per million opportunities**:

$$
DPMO = \frac{\text{defects}}{\text{units} \times \text{opportunities per unit}} \times 10^6
$$

| Sigma level | DPMO (with 1.5σ shift) | Yield |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | 308 537 | 69.1% |
| 3 | 66 807 | 93.3% |
| 4 | 6 210 | 99.38% |
| 5 | 233 | 99.977% |
| 6 | 3.4 | 99.99966% |

### Define

- Write a clear **problem statement** and **project charter**: scope, goals, business case, team, timeline.
- Identify customers and their **critical to quality (CTQ)** requirements through the **voice of the customer**.
- Map the process at high level with a **SIPOC** (suppliers, inputs, process, outputs, customers).

### Measure

- Define the output metric (Y) and how it will be measured.
- Validate the measurement system with **gauge R&R** (repeatability and reproducibility) studies; a poor measurement system can hide or invent problems.
- Collect baseline data and calculate current performance: yield, DPMO, sigma level and **process capability** indices Cp and Cpk.

### Analyse

- Identify potential causes (Xs) with **cause and effect (fishbone) diagrams**, process maps and 5 Whys, as in [root cause analysis methods](/safety-engineering/root-cause-analysis-methods/).
- Test which causes really matter using data: Pareto charts, scatter plots, **hypothesis tests**, regression and ANOVA.
- Express the relationship as Y = f(X₁, X₂, …).

### Improve

- Generate solutions that address verified root causes.
- Use **design of experiments (DOE)** to find optimal settings of key factors.
- Pilot solutions, check results statistically, assess risks with tools such as [FMEA](/safety-engineering/fmea-explained/), and implement.

### Control

- Standardise the new method with procedures, training and mistake proofing.
- Monitor key inputs and outputs with [control charts](/manufacturing-engineering/control-charts-spc/).
- Write a **control plan** with reaction rules, hand over to the process owner and close the project with documented savings.

| Quantity | Formula | Symbols and units |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Defects per million opportunities | DPMO = defects ÷ (units × opportunities per unit) × 10⁶ | Counts, result in defects per million |
| Sigma level | Read from the standard DPMO table with the 1.5σ shift | 3.4 DPMO corresponds to 6σ, 233 DPMO to 5σ, 6210 DPMO to 4σ |

## A worked DMAIC story

Consider a plant where 4% of moulded housings are rejected for short shots, parts not completely filled with plastic.

- **Define:** the team writes a charter to cut short shot rejects from 4% to below 1% within four months, worth a known annual saving in scrap and rework. The customer CTQ is a fully formed housing with no visible defects.
- **Measure:** they confirm that inspectors agree on what counts as a short shot through an attribute agreement study, then collect a month of data by machine, mould cavity, shift and material lot.
- **Analyse:** a Pareto chart shows most rejects come from two cavities of one mould. A fishbone session lists possible causes, and data show rejects rise when barrel temperature drops and when one material supplier is used. A hypothesis test confirms the supplier difference is significant.
- **Improve:** a designed experiment varies melt temperature, injection speed and hold pressure. The best settings plus cleaning a partly blocked vent in the two cavities bring rejects to 0.6% in a pilot run.
- **Control:** new settings go into the machine recipe with locked parameters, vent cleaning is added to the preventive maintenance plan, and a p chart of reject rate is posted at the machine with a reaction plan.

The result is documented, savings are confirmed by finance, and the process owner takes over monitoring.

## Common pitfalls

- Choosing projects that are too large or vague to finish in a few months.
- Jumping to solutions in the Define phase before data confirm the causes.
- Skipping measurement system analysis and chasing noise.
- Declaring success without a control plan, so the process drifts back.
- Treating Six Sigma as a statistics exercise rather than a team effort with the people who run the process.

## Roles in Six Sigma

- **Champion or sponsor:** senior manager who selects projects and removes barriers.
- **Master Black Belt:** expert coach and trainer.
- **Black Belt:** full time project leader skilled in advanced statistics.
- **Green Belt:** part time project leader or team member.
- **Yellow Belt:** team member with basic training.
- **Process owner:** responsible for sustaining results.

## Common tools by phase

| Phase | Typical tools |
| --- | --- |
| Define | Project charter, SIPOC, voice of customer, CTQ tree |
| Measure | Process map, data collection plan, gauge R&R, capability analysis |
| Analyse | Fishbone, Pareto, 5 Whys, hypothesis tests, regression |
| Improve | Brainstorming, DOE, pilot runs, FMEA, poka-yoke |
| Control | Control charts, control plan, standard work, audits |

## Making Six Sigma DMAIC work

Success depends on choosing projects linked to business goals and customer needs, leadership support, good data and disciplined tollgate reviews. Projects typically take three to six months. Combined with lean tools that remove the [seven wastes](/manufacturing-engineering/seven-wastes-lean/), DMAIC gives organisations a powerful, repeatable way to improve quality and reduce cost.

## Six Sigma DMAIC interview and viva questions

1. **What do the letters DMAIC stand for?** Define, Measure, Analyse, Improve and Control, the five phases used to improve an existing process.
2. **Why is gauge R&R done in the Measure phase?** A poor measurement system can hide or invent problems, so repeatability and reproducibility are checked before baseline data are trusted.
3. **What does 3.4 DPMO mean?** It is the defect rate of a six sigma process, about 3.4 defects per million opportunities when the 1.5σ long term shift is included.
4. **Which tools are typical of the Analyse phase?** Fishbone diagrams, Pareto charts, 5 Whys, hypothesis tests and regression, used to find and verify root causes.
5. **What is the role of a Black Belt?** A Black Belt is a full time project leader skilled in advanced statistics, coached by a Master Black Belt and supported by a Champion.
