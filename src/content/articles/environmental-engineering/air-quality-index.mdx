<Key title="Short answer">

The **air quality index** (AQI) is a number from 0 to 500 that converts measured concentrations of the main air pollutants into one health-based value. Each pollutant gets a sub-index from breakpoint tables using linear interpolation, and the reported AQI is the highest sub-index, shown with a colour and category.

</Key>

The air quality index is how most people meet air pollution data: a number on a weather app or a coloured board at a city junction. Behind it are continuous monitoring stations, averaging rules and breakpoint tables set by environmental agencies.

## Pollutants in the air quality index

The US AQI covers the five pollutants regulated by the US national ambient air quality standards:

- Ground-level ozone (O3), usually as an 8-hour average.
- Particulate matter: PM2.5 (particles up to 2.5 µm) and PM10 (up to 10 µm), as 24-hour averages.
- Carbon monoxide (CO), 8-hour average.
- Sulfur dioxide (SO2), usually 1-hour average.
- Nitrogen dioxide (NO2), 1-hour average.

The Indian National Air Quality Index, launched by the Central Pollution Control Board in 2014, covers eight: PM10, PM2.5, NO2, SO2, CO, O3, ammonia (NH3) and lead (Pb). An Indian AQI is reported only when data for at least three pollutants are available, and one of them must be PM2.5 or PM10.

Averaging periods matter because health effects depend on exposure time. A one-hour spike of particulate matter does not mean the 24-hour average is high, so agencies use rules such as the EPA NowCast to give hourly particle readings that track the 24-hour index.

For each pollutant, the agency publishes a **breakpoint table**: ranges of concentration that match ranges of index value. The sub-index is found by straight-line interpolation inside the range that contains the measured concentration:

$$
I_p = \frac{I_\text{Hi} - I_\text{Lo}}{BP_\text{Hi} - BP_\text{Lo}} \left( C_p - BP_\text{Lo} \right) + I_\text{Lo}
$$

where C_p is the truncated concentration of pollutant p, BP_Lo and BP_Hi are the breakpoints below and above it, and I_Lo and I_Hi are the index values at those breakpoints. The result is rounded to the nearest whole number.

The steps are:

1. Average the raw monitor data over the required period for each pollutant.
2. Truncate the concentration to the precision the table uses (for the US index, PM2.5 to one decimal place in µg/m³ and PM10 to a whole number).
3. Find the breakpoint range and calculate the sub-index.
4. Report the highest sub-index as the AQI and name that pollutant as the responsible pollutant.

Sub-indices are never added. A day with three pollutants at a sub-index of 90 still has an AQI of 90.

## US EPA air quality index categories and breakpoints

The US index has six categories, each with a fixed colour. In May 2024 the EPA revised the PM2.5 breakpoints after it tightened the annual PM2.5 standard to 9.0 µg/m³.

| AQI | Category | Colour | PM2.5, 24 h (µg/m³) | PM10, 24 h (µg/m³) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 to 50 | Good | Green | 0.0 to 9.0 | 0 to 54 |
| 51 to 100 | Moderate | Yellow | 9.1 to 35.4 | 55 to 154 |
| 101 to 150 | Unhealthy for sensitive groups | Orange | 35.5 to 55.4 | 155 to 254 |
| 151 to 200 | Unhealthy | Red | 55.5 to 125.4 | 255 to 354 |
| 201 to 300 | Very unhealthy | Purple | 125.5 to 225.4 | 355 to 424 |
| 301 to 500 | Hazardous | Maroon | 225.5 to 325.4 | 425 to 604 |

An AQI of 100 generally matches the short-term national ambient standard for that pollutant, which is why values above 100 start to carry warnings for sensitive groups.

## Indian National Air Quality Index

The CPCB index uses the same interpolation but its own categories and breakpoints.

| AQI | Category | PM2.5, 24 h (µg/m³) | PM10, 24 h (µg/m³) | NO2, 24 h (µg/m³) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 to 50 | Good | 0 to 30 | 0 to 50 | 0 to 40 |
| 51 to 100 | Satisfactory | 31 to 60 | 51 to 100 | 41 to 80 |
| 101 to 200 | Moderately polluted | 61 to 90 | 101 to 250 | 81 to 180 |
| 201 to 300 | Poor | 91 to 120 | 251 to 350 | 181 to 280 |
| 301 to 400 | Very poor | 121 to 250 | 351 to 430 | 281 to 400 |
| 401 to 500 | Severe | above 250 | above 430 | above 400 |

The Indian bands are tied to India's own national ambient air quality standards; an index of 100 for PM2.5 matches its 24-hour standard of 60 µg/m³. The two indices therefore cannot be compared number for number, as the example shows.

## Key formulas for the air quality index

| Quantity | Formula | Symbols and units |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pollutant sub-index | Ip = (IHi − ILo) ÷ (BPHi − BPLo) × (Cp − BPLo) + ILo | Cp = truncated concentration; BP = breakpoints in the same units; I = index values at the breakpoints |
| Reported AQI | AQI = max(Ip) | Highest sub-index of all pollutants measured |
| Slope within a band | (IHi − ILo) ÷ (BPHi − BPLo) | Index points per unit of concentration |

## Limits of the air quality index

The AQI is a communication tool, not a full exposure assessment. It reports one pollutant at a time and ignores combined effects. It describes outdoor air near the monitor, which may differ from indoor air or a busy roadside. The 2021 WHO air quality guidelines are stricter than most national standards, so a Good or Satisfactory index does not mean pollution is at the WHO guideline level.

For engineers, the AQI sits at the end of a chain that starts at emission sources. ## Air quality index interview and viva questions

1. **What does the air quality index represent?** It converts concentrations of several pollutants into one number on a 0 to 500 scale linked to health effects, with a category and colour for the public.
2. **How is a pollutant sub-index calculated?** By linear interpolation between the two breakpoints that bracket the measured concentration, using the index values assigned to those breakpoints.
3. **How is the overall AQI found from the sub-indices?** The highest sub-index is reported as the AQI, and that pollutant is named as the responsible pollutant; sub-indices are not added.
4. **What minimum data does the Indian National AQI need?** Data for at least three pollutants, one of which must be PM2.5 or PM10.
5. **Why can the same air give different AQI values in the US and India?** The two systems use different breakpoint tables and category bands, so the same concentration maps to a different index value.
