<Key title="Short answer">

**How a quadcopter flies:** four fixed pitch **propellers** on **brushless motors** push air downward to make thrust. Two spin clockwise and two counterclockwise so their reaction torques cancel. A **flight controller** reads gyroscopes, accelerometers and GPS and changes each motor's speed through **ESCs** to climb, pitch, roll and yaw.

</Key>

Multirotor drones are used for photography, mapping, inspection of power lines and wind turbines, agriculture spraying, search and rescue and delivery. Unlike aeroplanes, quadcopters have no control surfaces: all control comes from varying the speed of fixed pitch propellers. This makes them mechanically simple but completely dependent on fast electronics and control software.

- **Frame:** carbon fibre, glass fibre or plastic arms and body; X, H or plus layouts. Stiffness reduces vibration reaching the sensors.
- **Brushless DC (BLDC) motors:** rated by **kV** (rpm per volt) and size; low kV motors swing large propellers efficiently, high kV motors spin small racing propellers. Motor principles relate to [servo vs stepper motor](/automation-robotics/servo-vs-stepper-motor/) and other electronically commutated motors.
- **Electronic speed controllers (ESCs):** drive each motor's three phases with fast MOSFET switching, receiving speed commands from the flight controller via PWM, Oneshot or digital protocols such as DShot.
- **Propellers:** fixed pitch, specified by diameter and pitch (for example 10 × 4.5 inches); two clockwise and two counterclockwise.
- **Flight controller:** a microcontroller board, as in [microcontroller vs microprocessor](/electronics-engineering/microcontroller-vs-microprocessor/), running firmware such as PX4, ArduPilot or Betaflight.
- **Sensors:** **IMU** (3 axis gyroscope and accelerometer), **barometer** (altitude), **magnetometer** (heading), **GNSS receiver** (position), and optional optical flow, rangefinders, LiDAR and obstacle avoidance cameras.
- **Battery:** lithium polymer (LiPo) or lithium-ion packs, described by cell count (for example 4S = 14.8 V nominal), capacity (mAh) and discharge rating (C). A **power distribution board** and voltage regulators feed the electronics.
- **Radio control and telemetry:** receiver for pilot commands, telemetry link to a ground station, and video transmitter for first person view.
- **Payload:** camera on a stabilised gimbal, multispectral sensor, LiDAR, sprayer tank or delivery mechanism.

## Thrust and hover

Each propeller produces thrust roughly proportional to the square of its rotational speed:

$$
T = k\,\omega^2
$$

To hover, total thrust equals weight: 4T = mg. Good designs have a **thrust to weight ratio** of about 2:1 at full throttle, so hover needs about 50% throttle, leaving margin for control, wind and climbing.

From momentum theory, the ideal power to hover for a rotor of disc area A is:

$$
P_\text{ideal} = \sqrt{\frac{T^3}{2\rho A}}
$$

Real power is higher, divided by a **figure of merit** (about 0.5 to 0.7 for small propellers) and motor and ESC efficiencies. Larger, slower propellers need less power for the same thrust, which is why long endurance drones use large propellers.

## How a quadcopter flies: roll, pitch, yaw and throttle

- **Throttle (climb and descend):** increase or decrease all four motors equally.
- **Pitch (forward and back):** speed up the rear motors and slow the front ones to tilt nose down; the tilted thrust vector pushes the drone forward.
- **Roll (left and right):** speed up one side and slow the other to tilt and move sideways.
- **Yaw (rotate):** speed up the clockwise pair and slow the counterclockwise pair (or vice versa); the unbalanced reaction torque turns the drone, while total thrust stays constant.

These rotations are about the same three axes described for aircraft in [four forces of flight and aircraft axes](/aerospace-engineering/forces-of-flight-and-axes/).

## The flight control loop

The flight controller runs **cascaded control loops**:

1. **State estimation:** fuses gyro, accelerometer, magnetometer, barometer and GPS data, often with an extended Kalman filter, to estimate attitude, velocity and position.
2. **Rate loop:** the fastest loop (often 1 to 8 kHz) controls angular rates with PID controllers.
3. **Attitude loop:** controls roll, pitch and yaw angles by setting rate targets.
4. **Velocity and position loops:** in GPS modes, compute attitude targets to hold position, follow waypoints or return home.
5. **Mixer:** converts desired thrust and torques into four individual motor commands.

Flight modes range from **manual (acro)** to **stabilised**, **altitude hold**, **position hold** and fully **autonomous missions**. Tuning PID gains is similar to process loop tuning in industry.

## Key formulas for how a quadcopter flies

| Quantity | Formula | Symbols and units |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Propeller thrust | T = kω² | k = thrust constant, ω = rotational speed (rad/s), T in N |
| Hover condition | 4T = mg | m = total mass (kg) |
| Rotor disc area | A = πr² | r = propeller radius (m) |
| Ideal hover power per rotor | P_ideal = √(T³ ÷ (2ρA)) | ρ air density (kg/m³), P in W |
| Electrical hover power | P_elec = P_ideal ÷ (FM × η) | FM = figure of merit, η = motor and ESC efficiency |
| Hover time | t = E_usable ÷ P_total | E in Wh, P in W, t in h |

## Safety and regulations

Drones must be flown within national rules covering registration, pilot certification, maximum altitude, no fly zones near airports and sensitive areas, visual line of sight and operations over people. In India, the Drone Rules 2021 and the Digital Sky platform govern registration and permissions. Engineering safety features include geofencing, return to home on signal loss or low battery, redundant sensors and motors on larger drones, and parachutes for operations over people.

## Beyond the quadcopter

Hexacopters and octocopters add redundancy and payload capacity. **Fixed wing** drones fly longer and faster using wing lift, as in [how wings generate lift](/aerospace-engineering/how-wings-generate-lift/), and **VTOL hybrids** combine vertical takeoff with efficient wing borne cruise. Applications in farming are covered in [precision agriculture technologies](/agricultural-engineering/precision-agriculture-technologies/). Understanding how a quadcopter flies, through thrust, torque balance and fast feedback control, is the foundation for all of these designs.

## Quadcopter interview and viva questions

1. **Why do two propellers spin clockwise and two counterclockwise?** So that the reaction torques of the motors cancel in hover; changing the balance between the pairs is how the drone yaws.
2. **How does a quadcopter move forward?** The rear motors speed up and the front motors slow down, tilting the nose down so the tilted thrust vector pushes the drone forward.
3. **What is the role of an ESC?** It drives the three phases of a brushless motor with fast MOSFET switching, following speed commands from the flight controller.
4. **Why is a thrust to weight ratio of about 2:1 used?** Hover then needs only about 50% throttle, leaving margin for control, wind and climbing.
5. **Why do long endurance drones use large propellers?** Momentum theory shows that a larger disc area needs less power for the same thrust.
